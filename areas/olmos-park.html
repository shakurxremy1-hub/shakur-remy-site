Olmos Park

A small, quiet city of custom homes on large wooded lots, wrapped by parkland and minutes from the Pearl.

Typical range $800K–$5M Status Its own municipality Lots Large, wooded Vibe Private, established

The overview

Olmos Park is a compact incorporated city — well under a square mile — tucked between Olmos Basin parkland and the neighborhoods around the Pearl and the Quarry. It has its own city hall and its own police department, and the community deliberately keeps things low-key: light commercial footprint, quiet streets, and a strong preservation instinct.

What you get for that is space and calm inside the city. Lots are large and heavily treed, setbacks are deep, and yet you’re a few minutes from the Pearl’s restaurants, the McNay, Brackenridge Park, and a short hop to downtown or the airport.

The homes

The housing stock ranges from 1930s and 1940s estates — Spanish, Colonial Revival, and stately traditional — to full contemporary rebuilds on the original grounds. Because lots are big and the location is irreplaceable, land value is a large part of the price. Renovated period homes and new custom builds sit at the top of the range.

Schools

Addresses fall within San Antonio ISD, and many families here also weigh nearby Alamo Heights ISD homes or the private schools clustered in this part of town (Saint Mary’s Hall, TMI, Keystone, and others). If schooling drives your decision, tell me early and we’ll filter for it.

What to know before you buy or sell

  • Olmos Park enforces its own city ordinances closely — parking, permits, and traffic rules included.
  • Older homes on large lots can mean older systems, mature-tree root issues, and drainage to check.
  • Turnover is low; when the right home lists it draws attention, so be ready to move.
  • Sellers: the buyer pool is small but motivated and pays for condition, privacy, and correct restoration.
