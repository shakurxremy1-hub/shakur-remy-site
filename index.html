Good to know

Questions buyers and sellers ask.

How is my agent paid when I buy?

Since 2024, buyers sign a written representation agreement that states the agent’s compensation before touring homes. That fee is negotiable and may be paid by the seller, by you, or split — we go over the numbers up front so there are no surprises at closing.

What is the Texas option period?

For a small option fee, the buyer gets a set number of days to terminate the contract for any reason. It’s when inspections happen. If anything material turns up, you can renegotiate or walk and get your earnest money back.

How much earnest money do I need?

In the San Antonio market it’s commonly around 1% of the purchase price, held by the title company and credited to you at closing. Stronger earnest money can make an offer more competitive on a sought-after home.

What does it cost to sell?

Typically: negotiated agent compensation, title and escrow fees, prorated property taxes, and sometimes a survey, HOA transfer, or buyer concessions. I give you a net-proceeds estimate before we list and update it with every offer.

Do I need to be pre-approved before touring luxury homes?

Yes. Many higher-end listings require a lender pre-approval or proof of funds before a showing is confirmed. It also lets us move fast when the right home appears.

How long does a purchase take?

About 30–45 days from accepted offer to closing when financed, faster for cash. New construction runs on the builder’s timeline.

What are property taxes like here?

Texas has no state income tax; property tax rates in Bexar County generally run a little over 2% of assessed value depending on the taxing entities. A homestead exemption lowers the taxable value on your primary residence.

Can you represent me on a new-construction home?

Yes, and it’s worth having representation — the builder’s contract is written to protect the builder. I review the agreement, incentives, upgrades, and timeline with you.
